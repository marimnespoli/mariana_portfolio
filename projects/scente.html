The Challenge

Turn a first website into a consistent product, without losing its warmth.

The first version of Scenté had the right mood: deep browns, cream backgrounds, a serif paired with a monospace and a warm autumn collection. But it was a single landing page with loose spacing and no shared components. I refined it, designed the rest of the store and put one design system behind every screen, so the editorial feel holds from the home page to the cart.

The Context

Looking at the original page closely, the problems were small but everywhere: spacing that changed from section to section, text sizes that didn't follow a scale, a product grid that wasn't on the grid, and nothing reusable for a second page.

The brand itself was working, so the goal was never a redesign. Same colors, same fonts, same content. The work was in the structure underneath.

How might we keep everything that made Scenté feel warm, while making it consistent enough to grow into a full store and hand off to developers?

Design Decisions

Refinement Polish, not redesign

A 12 column grid, one spacing scale (120 between sections, 64 from title to content) and one type scale, applied to the brand that already existed.

Products Editorial product display

Big images with the name and price below, no divider lines. The same calm layout carries from the Shop to the Cart.

Consistency One rule for every shape

Every box, card and image shares one 8px radius, and every button is a pill. Small details, but they make the pages feel like one store.

The Cart

Forgiving Remove with undo

Removing an item shows a short toast with Undo, which puts it back in the same place. The minus button is disabled at one item instead of removing it by accident.

Focus Checkout always in reach

The order summary stays sticky on desktop and becomes a fixed checkout bar on mobile. The promo code field stays hidden until someone needs it.

Trust Answers before paying

Processing time and the returns window from Scenté's own policy sit right under the checkout button, and the suggestions stay smaller than the items in the cart.

On Mobile

  • 4 column grid20px margins, 16px gutters and 64px between sections, with a mobile type scale for headings.
  • Made for thumbsProduct rows and categories scroll sideways, the form stacks, and Add to cart is always visible since there is no hover on touch.
  • Same componentsA mobile navbar and footer join the system; everything else is the same components, resized.

The Design System

  • TokensColor, spacing and radius variables with CSS names, plus text styles for every heading, body and label size.
  • ComponentsNavbar, footer, buttons, product cards, cart item, quantity stepper, order summary, form fields and FAQ, with variants and working hovers.
  • HandoffEvery page is built from those components on the grid, with annotations for developers and a clickable prototype.

How I Used AI

Details Alignment and spacing

I used Claude to check alignment and spacing across sections, the small things that are easy to miss when you look at the same page for hours.

Exploration Testing ideas first

Before going to Figma, I explored pages like Contact and Cart in a quick canvas, reviewed the options and kept only what fit the brand.

Speed Faster work in Figma

It helped me build the variables, components, mobile versions and wireframes faster, so I could spend more time on the design decisions myself.
