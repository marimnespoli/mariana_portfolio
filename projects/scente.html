Scenté

Scenté is a candle brand I made up. I designed and coded its first website in HTML and CSS, then came back to it later to design the whole store in Figma.

The Challenge

Invent a small candle brand and give it a store that feels like autumn.

I wanted to design an online store from scratch, so I created the brand too. Scenté starts with one collection, an autumn one: Pumpkin Spice, Apple Cider, Vanilla Cashmere. Everything else came from that feeling of lighting a candle on a cold evening.

The Story

Scenté isn't a real company. I invented it so I could design a store from the brand up, without anyone telling me what it should look like. The idea was two friends who started making candles together, and that became the About page.

Since the first collection is for autumn, the colors came from the season: deep brown, warm cream, a rusty red for the headings and a soft butter yellow for the buttons. For the type I mixed an elegant serif with a monospace, so it feels a little handmade, like a label on a jar.

My first version was a single landing page I coded myself. Later I came back to it and designed the rest of the store: About, Shop, the product page, Contact and Cart, on desktop and mobile.

Who It's For

I created two personas, based on the two main reasons people buy candles. Both have the same problem: you can’t smell a candle through a screen.

Chloé, the gift giver

“I want a gift that feels thoughtful, but I can’t smell it through the screen.” She needs scents named after real ingredients, clear prices and delivery times before checkout.

Priya, the evening ritual

“After a long shift I light a candle to switch off. I want to know what I’m breathing in.” She needs ingredients in plain words, care tips and the story of who makes the candles.

Moodboard and Identity

Before designing, I collected references to find the mood of the brand: autumn light, real ingredients and amber glass. The palette, the photos and the type of the store all came from this board.

Sitemap and User Flows

The product page is new: it's where people decide to buy. Chloé decides by the scent notes and checks delivery times in the cart. Priya reads the ingredients on the product page and confirms them in the FAQ.

Details I Cared About

Photos first

Candles sell by how they look and feel, so the product photos are big and the name and price sit quietly below them.

A calm cart

Removing an item shows a small Undo, the order summary stays in view, and shipping and returns info sit right under the checkout button.

One store, six pages

The same header, footer, buttons and spacing on every page, so going from Home to Cart feels like the same place.

Wireframes

I started every page in grey, to get the layout right before the photos and colors came in.

The Store

Six pages, from the autumn collection on the home page to the cart. On the product page, big photos show the candle next to its ingredients, and the price, Add to cart and Buy now are all at the top. Scroll inside the window to see each page.

On Mobile

On the phone, the order summary turns into a checkout bar fixed at the bottom of the cart, so it's always one tap away.

The Design System

The colors, type, spacing and components live in Figma, so every new page starts from the same pieces.

How I Used AI

The small stuff

Checking alignment and spacing across sections, the things that are easy to miss after looking at the same page for hours.

Trying ideas

Before going to Figma, I tried different layouts for pages like Contact and Cart in a quick canvas and kept only what fit the brand.

Moving faster

It helped me build the components, mobile versions and wireframes in Figma faster, so I had more time for the design decisions.
